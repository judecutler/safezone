# SafeZone story and verified facts

Researched Nov 4, 2026. Every number below was read from the source linked next to it in this session. Wording is paraphrased. Quote nothing longer than a short phrase from these sources.

## Correction before anything else

The Dec 5, 2024 warning was not at night. The earthquake struck at 10:44 a.m. Pacific on a Thursday, and the warning was cancelled at 11:54 a.m. Any script line that says "that night" is wrong.

## The story (illustration, not a real family)

> *Illustration. The family below is invented. The event, the times and the official guidance are real.*

On Thursday, Dec 5, 2024, at 10:44 a.m., a magnitude 7.0 earthquake struck off Cape Mendocino. Seven minutes later, phones across San Francisco buzzed with a tsunami warning, one of several million people covered from Davenport to southern Oregon. Picture the Reyes family, two blocks from Ocean Beach in the Outer Sunset: a grandmother who uses a wheelchair, a six-year-old, and a dog. The alert says to move away from coastal waters. It doesn't say whether their block is in the hazard area, which way to walk, or how long it takes. No siren sounds, because the city's sirens had been off since 2019. Neighbors walk down to the beach to watch, and the forecast first wave is due at 12:10 p.m. In the SafeZone version of that morning, the Reyes family already made their plan weeks earlier. The app checked their address against California's official tsunami hazard map and flagged it inside. It worked out a walking route out of the zone, with a slow pace for the grandmother, and it listed only wheelchair-accessible shelters. The phone stored all of it, so it works without a network. When the alert arrives, they open SafeZone and follow the route. Nobody has to decide anything new, and nobody heads toward the water. (That day, the warning was cancelled at 11:54 a.m. and no damaging wave came. The next warning may not end the same way.)

One fact matters for the voice-over. For a local quake, official guidance says to go when you feel strong or long shaking, without waiting for an alert. The Dec 2024 quake was distant enough that San Francisco felt little shaking, so the alert was the trigger. SafeZone covers both cases, and its go-now screen already says not to wait for an official warning.

## The Kamaishi lesson, stated carefully

In Kamaishi, Iwate, on March 11, 2011, nearly all of the city's roughly 3,000 elementary and junior high students survived the tsunami. Japan's government reports 2,911 who escaped and five who died, and those five were not at school that day. It gives the students' survival rate as 99.8%. The difference was preparation done years earlier. From 2004 the city's board of education worked with Professor Toshitaka Katada of Gunma University. Schools set aside about ten hours a year for disaster lessons and drilled evacuation. In 2010 they finished a tsunami manual used in all 14 schools. On the day, students at Unosumai ran to the planned evacuation site. When a local resident warned them it wasn't safe, they kept going uphill, and the older children led the younger ones. Katada's three principles were: don't trust assumptions blindly (the hazard map is a projection), do the best you can in the moment, and be the first to evacuate. Families were taught *tsunami tendenko*: each person evacuates on their own and trusts the others to do the same, instead of going back to search.

**How to connect it to SafeZone.** The claim is not that an app would have saved anyone in Kamaishi. The claim is narrower. People who settle what to do before the emergency act faster in it, and the learning was done before the day it was needed. SafeZone moves that preparation into a household: prepare once, then follow the plan. Give the plan two of Kamaishi's lessons as well. The route treats the official map as a floor, not a ceiling: at Unosumai, 86% of the dead were in homes outside the mapped hazard area. Each household member can also follow the plan on their own, without waiting for the others.

Things not to say. Don't say "SafeZone would have saved the Kamaishi children" or that it "brings the Kamaishi miracle to California". Don't say "99.8% survived because of drills" as a measured cause. The drills are the documented explanation, not a controlled result.

## Real data lines for the app

Each line is under 20 words, and each source link goes next to it in the UI.

1. "Dec 5, 2024: about 5 million people got a tsunami warning. It didn't show who was in the zone." Sources: [KQED](https://www.kqed.org/news/12032295) and the [Berkeley report](https://dev.berkeleyca.gov/sites/default/files/documents/2025-04-18%20%202024%20Tsunami%20Warning%20-%20After%20Action%20Report%20and%20Community%20Survey%20Summary.pdf).
2. "In recent Japanese earthquakes, 30–50% of injuries came from furniture tipping, falling or sliding." Source: [Tokyo Fire Department](https://www.tfd.metro.tokyo.lg.jp/content/000010472.pdf).
3. "Only 35% of Californians say they are prepared for a disaster." Source: [PPIC, 2024](https://www.ppic.org/blog/few-californians-are-prepared-for-a-disaster/).
4. "Kamaishi, 2011: years of drills, and nearly all of the city's 3,000 schoolchildren reached high ground." Source: [Government of Japan](https://www.atlanta.us.emb-japan.go.jp/hts5.html).
5. "Noto, 2024: the first tsunami wave reached the coast within minutes, before the warning." Source: [nippon.com](https://nippon.com/en/in-depth/d00986).

Line 1 suits the landing page. Line 2 suits the room-scan result, and line 5 suits the go-now screen, next to "do not wait for an official warning".

## Could not verify, so do not repeat

- Any Dec 2024 evacuation share (how many left, how many went to the beach, how many drove). No source gives percentages. Berkeley's survey reports "many" and "numerous", not numbers.
- Traffic jams in San Francisco itself on Dec 5. The documented traffic jams were in Berkeley and on I-80, and BART's Transbay Tube closed. Don't say the Sunset was gridlocked.
- A San Francisco Chronicle article titled "'You are in danger!': Mad chaos as San Francisco scrambled" turned up in search but would not load. Don't cite it.
- The often-repeated "5.3 million" count. It comes from AP citing USGS, but other outlets say 4.6 million or nearly 5 million. Say "about 5 million".
- "40,000 San Franciscans live in a tsunami hazard zone". It's a Datawrapper chart (CGS plus Census data) whose publisher and method weren't checked. It names Mission Bay, the Marina and the Financial District as the most exposed, not Ocean Beach. Don't use it for an Ocean Beach claim.
- Ocean Beach's hazard status. Only SafeZone's own CGS check says 1000 Great Highway is inside. The CGS 2021 release mentions only minor changes at Ocean Beach, and the SF Zoo grounds may flood in extreme events. The CGS map is a worst-case scenario at roughly a thousand-year return period. Call it "the official tsunami hazard area", not "where the wave will go".
- The Northridge "55% of injuries" figure. It came from a secondhand ECA page, and the original UCLA study wasn't read. The peer-reviewed Peek-Asa 1998 abstract only says most hospitalized injuries were falls or being hit by objects, and that most deaths were from collapse.
- Whether securing furniture measurably cuts injuries. Nobody found a measured effect size. Say "most injuries come from contents, so securing them targets the main cause", not "securing furniture cuts injuries by X%".
- Katada's own objection to the "miracle" label (釜石の出来事 rather than 奇跡). It is widely reported, but no page read here quoted him on it. Say "often called the Kamaishi miracle" without attributing a view to him.
- How many Kamaishi students ran and how far. Sources give 600 students, then 700 m and 500 m legs, or "over 500 students". Don't state a single precise distance.
- A study that measures survival, not timing, by prior knowledge of routes. None was found. Unosumai shows the opposite risk: most of the dead were outside the mapped zone.
- Noto 2024 death toll and how many died in the tsunami versus building collapse. Not checked.
- California's "40% have a family plan" figure. A Cal OES fact sheet says it, but its survey year isn't stated, so it isn't used.
- Voice guidance. The voice-led emergency is planned on the `redesign` branch (`docs/redesign.md`), but the `web/js` in this checkout has no speech code. Say "voice-led" in the video or on Devpost only once it has merged and the browser check covers it.
